# Your Equinox scan already outruns public Ultium knowledge

The pivot from an AI gas-car diagnosis app to a read-only EV/hybrid battery-health toolkit was the right call for this project's first goal, the applied-AI portfolio, and a neutral-to-slightly-worse call for income. Neither direction was ever going to produce more than modest money. On the technical side, the news is better than the project's own Gate B status suggests. Public Ultium knowledge is thin: OBDb has four DIDs, and the rest comes from a few community repos, all from August–September 2026. Your 2026-09-23 discovery recording already goes beyond it on the Equinox. It also contains, unwatched, the two signals Gate B is looking for. On the drive motor module `0x17`, DID `2414` answers in the default session: it reads pack current on a Lyriq and a Hummer EV. On the same module, DID `2885` reads **326.49 V** on your car, which agrees with your own cell-sum estimate of 326.2 V. The next hardware session is therefore a short, targeted charge watch of those two DIDs, not another broad search. Commercially, a Ultium app is realistically a **low-thousands to low-tens-of-thousands of dollars per year** product. Getting to the US$15–30k/yr "base case" takes in-person GTA inspections or dealer work on top of app sales. Consumers and dealers already get free or cheap substitutes: Car Scanner, Recurrent, and in the GTA, Lyteflo.

## The public Ultium DID map fits on one page

No mature public Ultium PID set exists. OBDb's Equinox EV signalset defines exactly four Mode 22 commands. Three are on the BECM (`DACB`): high-resolution SOC `27C6`, cell voltage avg/min/max `2AF5`, and an 8-bit SOC `2B43`. The fourth, `33E5` on `DA1D`, is labelled a battery voltage but tops out at 25.5 V ([OBDb signalset](https://github.com/OBDb/Chevrolet-Equinox-EV/blob/main/signalsets/v3/default.json)). The same four appear in the Chevrolet, Cadillac, GMC and Buick make-level signalsets ([OBDb/Chevrolet](https://github.com/OBDb/Chevrolet/blob/main/signalsets/v3/default.json)). Everything else comes from three recent community efforts:

- two unmerged Lyriq PRs, which add DMCM pack current `2414` (s16 ÷20 A, negative = charging) and a set of ECU-40 temperature and group-voltage DIDs ([OBDb Lyriq PR #14](https://github.com/OBDb/Cadillac-LYRIQ/pull/14));
- a Flutter Lyriq reverse-engineering repo, which lists state-of-health (SOH) and capacity *candidates* on ECU `40` ([lyriq-did-map.md](https://github.com/Daniel085/OBD_Battery-diagnostics/blob/main/docs/lyriq-did-map.md));
- a 2024 Hummer EV project, the most rigorous of the three. It measured energy remaining (`27AF`, ÷100 kWh), range (`27C7`, ÷103 mi), pack voltage (`17`/`2885`, ÷100 V) and pack current (`17`/`2414`), and it tags every signal with an evidence level ([TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md)).

A WiCAN issue for the 2027 Bolt adds fitted formulas for charge-cycle counters (`27C0`, `27BB`, `27BF`) and module temperature (`2AF1`) ([wican-fw #884](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/wican-fw/issues/884)).

The same sources correct three common mislabels. **`33E5` is the 12 V rail, not HV**: it answers at 13–14 V on modules 17, 1D and 28. **`2429` is a bipolar torque/load signal** whose zero sits at 0x5806, not a nominal pack voltage. **`5401` is a charging-state enum, not charger power** ([PR #14](https://github.com/OBDb/Cadillac-LYRIQ/pull/14); [TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md)). No public source identifies GM's own SOH or capacity DID on any Ultium vehicle. On the Lyriq, the Bolt capacity DID `41A3` and Bolt SOC DIDs return NRC 0x31 ([lyriq-re-log.md](https://github.com/Daniel085/OBD_Battery-diagnostics/blob/main/docs/lyriq-re-log.md)). The Hummer project lists SOH as absent from the BSM ([GM_MODULE_MAP.md](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/GM_MODULE_MAP.md)).

The commercial side confirms the gap. Car Scanner's "Ultium" profile shows some live BMS data on the Equinox EV, but owners say many of its gauges stay empty ([Equinox EV Forum, snippet](https://www.equinoxevforum.com/threads/question-for-those-who-don%E2%80%99t-see-the-car-scanner-app.6907/)). A March 2026 guide says no app specifically supports Ultium or the Equinox EV ([VoltChek](https://voltchek.app/blog/ev-battery-health-check-app-guide)).

Two conclusions follow. First, the "no sign-flipping current" result in the discovery run is an artifact of the watch cap, not a finding. The phase-C watch list held 201 single-frame CB DIDs and **zero on module 17** (`docs/discovery-2026-09.md` §4). Both public current sources put current on DMCM `0x17`, not the BECM. Second, `27AF` ÷ SOC already gives a BMS-side usable-capacity figure: 74.91 kWh ÷ 0.847 ≈ **88 kWh**. That makes it a cheap reference line for T2.4's capacity estimate. It is a single-sample number, and the SOC basis (displayed or raw) is unknown, so treat it as a working value until several sessions agree.

A note on sourcing under hard rule 1: OBDb signalsets count as a source once they are checked into `packages/obd-core/vehicles/`. The Hummer and Lyriq repos do not count. Their formulas become citable only after your own recording reproduces them, which the table above now does for `2885`, `2429`, `27AF` and `27C6`.

## Next session: a ten-minute charge watch settles Gate B

The priority order follows from what is cheap, read-only, and decisive. **First**, run the T2.3b targeted watch on `18DA17F1`: `22 2414` and `22 2885`, plus CB `27AF`, `27C6` and `2AF7`, through at least five minutes of AC charging with the car in Ready. Pass condition: `2414` goes negative during charge. Then V × I should match the charger's power within a few percent. On the Hummer this came to 8.14 kW computed against 7.7 kW from the energy slope ([TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md §1b](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md)). Fix the tx-ordering bug in the recorder first; it currently fails replay at line 49776. Keep the car awake. On the Lyriq, unattended AC charging left ECU 40 sparse, and CB, 1D and 40 "sleep fast when idle" ([lyriq-re-log.md](https://github.com/Daniel085/OBD_Battery-diagnostics/blob/main/docs/lyriq-re-log.md)).

**Second**, check that `27AF` ÷ SOC stays constant through the charge. The Hummer's did: 191.8–191.9 kWh. If yours does too, the ratio works as a BMS capacity reading you can trend across sessions. **Third**, run a bounded Mode 22 sweep of module `40`. Your phase A shows `40` answering as the BCM. On the Lyriq, ECU `40` holds the SOH candidates `443C`, `4441` and `451D` (reading 100/100/99), a capacity-record candidate `44C5`, group voltages `416C`–`416E`, and pack temperatures `4124`/`4127` ([lyriq-did-map.md](https://github.com/Daniel085/OBD_Battery-diagnostics/blob/main/docs/lyriq-did-map.md); [PR #14](https://github.com/OBDb/Cadillac-LYRIQ/pull/14)). On the Hummer, module `40` answers only at CAN priority 0x18 ([CAN_PRIORITY.md](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/CAN_PRIORITY.md)). Your tool already uses priority 0x18 (`ATSH DA40F1`), and WiCAN's profiles use 0x14 ([bt1.json](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/wican-fw/blob/main/vehicle_profiles/bt1/bt1.json)). The per-module priority belongs in the vehicle profile, not in a global setting.

**Fourth**, time-series the single-byte runs and `2AF1` against coolant and ambient, to source a temperature scaling. The two public fits for `2AF1` disagree: WiCAN has °C = x − 80, the Hummer has (x − 40)/2.

Two things to leave alone. Do not attempt a `1003` session, SecurityAccess, or any 2E/31 service. The Hummer's security-locked DIDs sit on the drive units only ([SCAN_RESULTS](https://github.com/JeremyWhittaker/hummer_obdII/blob/main/docs/SCAN_RESULTS_2026-09-20.md)), and hard rule 5 rules them out anyway. Also, do not assume your 2024 results carry forward. OBDb's 2025 Equinox capture lists all three DACB DIDs as unsupported ([2025 command_support](https://github.com/OBDb/Chevrolet-Equinox-EV/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2025/command_support.yaml)). A 2025 Lyriq's BECM answers J1979 but no 22xx request, even after 12+ header and flow-control combinations ([lyriq-did-map.md](https://github.com/Daniel085/OBD_Battery-diagnostics/blob/main/docs/lyriq-did-map.md)). The app needs a clear "BECM silent on Mode 22" state. For MY2026+, add a functional `22 F4B2` probe. ZEVonUDS (J1979-3) standardizes a propulsion-battery SOH DID there, and it phases in from 40 % of MY2026 ZEVs ([Vector](https://www.vector.com/en/business-unit/testing-validation-diagnostics/diagnostics/standards-knowhow/zevonuds/); [Kvaser](https://kvaser.com/the-future-of-on-board-diagnostics-obd-on-uds-and-zev-on-uds/)). No Ultium vehicle has yet been reported answering it.

A cheap portfolio move is to upstream the `2AE1`–`2AE7` decode and the `2AF5` tail semantics to OBDb, with your test vectors. That turns private findings into public, reviewable evidence.

## The pivot traded a stable interface for a rarer skill story

The gas-car market was larger, not smaller. The US fleet averages a record **12.8 years across 289M vehicles** ([S&P Global Mobility](https://press.spglobal.com/2025-05-21-U-S-Vehicle-Age-Rises-Again-to-12-8-Years-in-2025,-According-to-S-P-Global-Mobility)). But "an LLM explains your code" is already commoditized. FIXD bundles an AI Mechanic into a roughly $70–100/yr subscription ([OBDadvisor](https://obdadvisor.com/fixd-vs-bluedriver/)). Apps such as OBDAI and "AI Mechanic" on Google Play compete on the same pitch ([OBDAI](https://obdai.app/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=mechsit.ai.obd2gpt&hl=en_US)). A solo developer would have been fighting incumbents with hardware distribution.

The pivot gave up three real things:

- **A legally mandated, stable data interface.** Mode 01 is required emissions data. The new plan depends on undocumented Mode 22 reads behind GM's Global B gateway, which GM can change in any model year ([diag.net](https://diag.net/msg/m10du7dhoyesvgeq01o1qvv9xu)).
- **An instantly legible demo.** "Check-engine light, AI tells you why" needs no explanation.
- **The induced-fault evaluation idea**, the one unusual asset in the old plan.

It gained a skill signal that is rarer and more marketable for an applied-AI portfolio:

- calibrated capacity intervals from noisy charge logs;
- degradation time series;
- on-device inference;
- an LLM layer held to a deterministic number check.

Battery SOH ML is a named, niche hiring category, for example Clarios's "AI/ML Battery SOH Engineer" ([JobLeads](https://www.jobleads.com/us/job/ai-ml-battery-soh-engineer-for-predictive-analytics--milwaukee--ee6b6d349cc45d587957f0425fc9d5ef3)). A 2026 paper finds that OEM battery-health reporting "fails independent validation across manufacturers" ([arXiv 2603.21592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21592)), which is exactly the case for independent measurement with honest uncertainty. The MCP server, eval harness and faithfulness check carry over whichever vehicle domain you pick.

Market timing favours the used-EV angle over new EVs. US new-EV share fell to about 5–6 % after the federal credit ended ([Cox, June 2026](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/ev-market-monitor-june-2026/)). Used EVs are growing: a record 128k in Q2 2026 ([GCN, aggregator](https://gcn.com/128-000-secondhand-electric-cars-sold/21727/)), with roughly 50k electric lease returns a month building through 2028 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/04/07/used-evs-just-hit-a-sales-record-a-much-bigger-wave-is-coming/)). Canada's ZEV share rebounded to 12.2 % in March 2026 under the new $5,000 EV Affordability Program ([Canadian Auto Dealer](https://canadianautodealer.ca/2026/09/are-ev-sales-rebounding/)).

The Ultium niche itself is weakening. Equinox EV US sales fell **41 % in H1 2026**, to 16,249 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/07/01/chevy-equinox-ev-sales-fall-41-new-bolt-picks-up-slack/)). GM took $7.6B in EV charges and dropped the Ultium brand name ([Hagerty](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/general-motors-ev-loss-2025/); [MotorAuthority](https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1144673_gm-reportedly-ditching-ultium-ev-branding-on-batteries-and-tech)). Batteries also mostly last: about 95 % of range after five years, with only 1.5 % of owners replacing a pack outside warranty ([InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/features/794674/used-ev-battery-health-guide-2026/)). The demand is for reassurance at purchase, not rescue.

The steelman for the gas-car path is legibility plus a data interface that cannot disappear. It loses because that interface's value has already been captured by others.

The verdict is conditional. The pivot is **good for the portfolio if and only if** a current or energy signal holds up on hardware. The evidence above says it very likely will. If it does not, the flagship ML result falls back to charger kWh or synthetic data. The project's own rules then require labelling it that way, and the showcase gets thinner.

## Realistic money: an app in the low thousands, inspections in the low tens of thousands

App pricing is anchored low. LeafSpy Pro, the best precedent for a single-model battery tool, sells for **$19.99 one-time**, and no ELM327 app has proven a price above ~$20 ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Turbo3.Leaf_Spy_Pro&hl=en_US)). Car Scanner has 10M+ installs, is free or ~$5–10 Pro, and already ships an Ultium profile ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/car-scanner-elm-obd2/com.ovz.carscanner)). OBD Fusion sells at $9.99 plus ~$9.99 per-make add-ons ([OBDadvisor](https://obdadvisor.com/obd-fusion-app-review/)). Freemium apps convert a median **2.1 %** of installs ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), and Google keeps 15 % ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)).

Recurrent gives shoppers free reports and covers the Equinox, Blazer and Lyriq, though from telematics and range data, not cell data ([Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/sell/chevrolet)). In the GTA, Lyteflo (Ontario, C$3M seed) already sells OBD-II battery certificates to dealers such as Carnex in Mississauga ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/lyteflo-secures-3-million-cad-in-seed-funding-to-rev-up-ev-sales-platform/)). Carnex's battery-health page, however, lists **no GM Ultium models** ([carnex.ca](https://carnex.ca/battery-health)). General GTA pre-purchase inspections cost about C$199 and already include EVs ([CarInspect](https://carinspect.com/car-inspection-toronto/)). Aviloo charges dealers €480/yr plus €35 per test ([Aviloo](https://aviloo.com/en/b2b-aviloo-flash-test-us)).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding here is that Gate B is probably already half-passed and simply unobserved. Module `0x17` answers the exact current and voltage DIDs that two other Ultium vehicles use. Its voltage matches your cell sum to 0.3 V, and its load signal sits at the Hummer's exact zero point. The recording is waiting for a charge event to show a sign flip. If `2414` flips, the project gets something no public tool has on the Equinox: signed current, pack voltage, BMS energy, and all 80 cell groups. That is enough for a coulomb-counted capacity estimate with a real interval, checked against the BMS's own `27AF`/SOC ratio. It is a genuinely publishable portfolio result.

The money question has a less comfortable answer. The market will pay for *trust at the moment of a used-EV sale*, and it does not pay much for software to get it. Treat the Ultium app as a credibility asset and a funnel. If income matters, it will come from occasional GTA inspections or a small dealer arrangement, not from Play Store sales. Keep the analysis layer vehicle-generic. MY2025+ gateway filtering and ZEVonUDS mean the Ultium-specific DID table is the part of the project with the shortest shelf life.
