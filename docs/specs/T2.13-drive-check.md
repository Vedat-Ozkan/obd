# T2.13: Drive check (cell groups under load, then a codes rescan)

## Goal

ADR-023 changes the drive check. Its headline is now each cell group's sag under load compared with the other 79 groups, followed by a codes rescan. It is no longer absolute pack resistance, which failed T2.12's G3 (`docs/test-drive-2026-09.md` §5). This task builds that check in three stages:

- **Stage A (desk)**, split into A1 and A2:
  - A1 builds the measure, writes the pass rule from drive 1 only, and commits that rule.
  - A2 tightens the capture schedule, re-sends a `7F 22 21` reply once, adds a 10-minute option, and runs the held-out replay of drive 2.
- **Stage B**: one confirming owner drive.
- **Stage C**: a separate drive report (owner Q6) holding the groups-under-load finding and an automatic codes rescan compared with the pre-drive scan. It can be built and desk-verified before Stage B; it labels the group rule as unconfirmed until Stage B passes.

What a test or user sees:
- `pnpm drive-capture` on either 2026-09-30 drive prints a `## Groups under load` section with no flagged group.
- The same section on drive 1 with one injected weak group flags exactly that group.
- The phone records 10- or 20-minute drives on the tighter schedule.
- After Stage C, a finished drive saves a drive report: the groups finding with its rule-status label, and a codes comparison against the earlier parked report of the same garage entry (or "no pre-drive scan to compare").

## Non-goals

- Showing absolute pack resistance anywhere in the app. It is recorded in the artifact only (ADR-023 item 4).
- Temperature bins or any °C value. Those wait for T2.17, and `2AF1` stays raw.
- An AI summary of the drive (ADR-023, Amends).
- The red-flag verdict and coverage list (T2.15). This task produces the drive finding; T2.15 maps it to an outcome.
- The guided flow, moving the drive button out of Developer tools, and the full parked-scan rerun after the drive (T2.18).
- Manufacturer codes via service `19` (T2.16). The Stage C rescan uses today's `CODES_SCAN_COMMANDS` only.
- Changes to `obd-core`, `obd-battery/src/session.ts`, the charge log's behaviour, or the T2.12 Steps/Intervals sections of `drive-capture`.
- Re-tuning the rule after drive 2 or the confirming drive (Rule, Split isolation).
- In Stage C: any change to `BatteryDiagnosisReport`, `redFlagScreen` or the T2.15/T2.18 specs; any verdict or outcome for the drive (T2.15 maps the finding later); reopening drive reports from the garage or linking them from the parked report screen (T2.18); the parked-scan rerun after the drive (T2.18); moving the drive button out of Developer tools; a "withdrawn" rule state (a follow-up only if Stage B fails and the owner picks "not checked"); showing `medianGroupMohm`; a new dependency.
- Any rest-voltage (intercept) comparison between groups. That is a parked-scan check (T2.14–T2.15). This task compares slopes only, so a constant sensor offset does not flag a group under load (docs/ML.md §BM3: an offset is constant, connection resistance scales with current).

## Design

### The measure (A1)

The measure is a pure function over the `ChargeLog` that `chargeLogFromRecording` already builds. Decoding stays T2.4's.

1. **Steady sets.** A complete group set `k` (`log.groups`, t = its `2AE1` tx) is *steady* when all of these hold:
   - the last current sample before it and the first after it both exist;
   - they are at most `BRACKET_MAX_S` apart;
   - they differ by at most `STEADY_A`;
   - no `log.recoveries` time falls between them.

   Its current is `I_k`, the mean of the two samples. Both schedules work, because no `2414` read sits inside the seven group reads in either the T2.12 schedule or the A2 schedule.
2. **Deviation from the set median.** `m_k` is the median of the 80 `volts` in set `k`, and `d_gk = volts_k[g] − m_k`. Subtracting the median removes the effects common to all groups: SOC drift during the drive, pack-level sag, and polarisation.
3. **Excess sag per amp.** For each group `g`, take the least-squares slope `b_g` of `d_gk` on `I_k` over the steady sets.
   - Excess `e_g = −1000·b_g` mΩ. Positive means the group sags further per amp than the median group.
   - Its standard error is `se_g` (mΩ, the usual OLS slope SE with n − 2 degrees of freedom).
   - The lower bound is `lb_g = e_g − Z·se_g`.
4. **Status.**
   - `not-checked` when fewer than `MIN_LOADED_SETS` steady sets have `|I_k| ≥ LOAD_A`, with that reason.
   - `not-checked` when any steady set's `modules` array differs from the first steady set's, reason "group order changed". Group identity is the record index, and T2.4 Decision 8 assumes the order is stable.
   - Otherwise `checked`.
5. **Flag.** Group `g` is flagged when `checked` and `lb_g > GROUP_EXCESS_LIMIT_MOHM`. The rule is one-sided, because a group that sags less than the others is not a red flag.
6. **Recorded, not shown.** `medianGroupMohm` is the median over groups of `−1000 ×` the slope of `volts_k[g]` (not the deviation) on `I_k`. It is the absolute per-group figure ADR-023 says to record. It goes into the artifact and into the Stage C report data, never into app text.

A group is named `group <g+1> (module <modules[g]>)`.

### Rule (A1): pre-registered here, L set from drive 1 only

Policy values. ADR-023 item 2 calls them "project policy". They are fixed in this spec **before any group statistic is computed on any drive**:

| Name | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `STEADY_A` | 10 A | T2.12's `BRACKET_SAME_A`. Drive 1 has 193 of 348 sets within it (findings §2, Skew). |
| `BRACKET_MAX_S` | 3.0 s | Above drive 1's maximum bracket of 2.545 s (findings §2), so it only excludes sets that sit across a stall. |
| `LOAD_A` | 50 A | A set "under load". About 50 × the ≈1 A idle draw in Ready (discovery §7.4). |
| `MIN_LOADED_SETS` | 10 | The fewest loaded steady sets for a slope. Below this the result is `not-checked`, not a pass. |
| `Z` | 2 | The lower-bound multiplier on the slope SE. |
| `MARGIN` | 2 | The multiplier in the formula for L. |

**`GROUP_EXCESS_LIMIT_MOHM` (L)** is computed once, in A1, from drive 1 (`2026-09-30-test-drive.redacted.jsonl`):

`L = ceil(100 × MARGIN × max_g e_g(drive 1)) / 100` mΩ

The implementer:
1. runs the A1 section on drive 1 with L unset (the section prints `max excess`);
2. writes L into `driveGroups.ts`, with a comment that cites drive 1, the printed max, and this formula;
3. pastes the drive-1 artifact into `docs/task-runs/T2.13.md`.

The orchestrator commits A1 (the **rule commit**) after review and before anything in A2.

**Split isolation** (docs/ML.md §Splits; hard rule 11):
- Drive 1 is the only data that sets L.
- Drive 2 is held out. No code from this task runs the group measure on drive 2 before the rule commit exists, and drive 2's test case is added in A2.
- After the rule commit, L and the six values above do not change in response to drive 2, the synthetic case or the Stage B drive. If any of them disagrees with the rule, that is reported as a FAIL and escalated (Risks), not tuned away.
- By construction drive 1 passes its own rule (`lb ≤ e ≤ max < L`). Its "no flag" therefore shows that the code ran. It is not evidence that the rule works, and the task record says so.

### Synthetic weak group (A1)

docs/EVAL.md ("faults exist only as synthetic injections into real logs") and docs/ML.md §BM3 (a resistance or connection rise scales with current) shape this case. A weak group is injected into **drive 1's** decoded log in memory, not into a file:

- `volts_k[g] −= (x/1000)·I(t_k)`, where `I(t_k)` is the current linearly interpolated from `log.current` at the set's t (the nearest sample when there is one on only one side).
- The injection applies to every complete set, steady or not.
- The log is marked `synthetic: true`.

Drive 2 stays untouched, so it remains clean held-out data.

- The injected group is the one at sorted position 39 of drive 1's `e_g`, the lower median.
- There are two cases: `x = 4·L` must flag exactly that group, and `x = 0.5·L` must flag nothing.
- The artifacts are labelled synthetic, and their results are never mixed into a real-data table.

Group reads in one set are about 0.7 s apart, so using `t_k` for every group is an approximation. It only affects the synthetic case.

### Capture schedule (A2)

T2.12's block put the CB setup and three context reads between the group reads and the currents around them. The bracket's median before-gap was 0.46 s and its median after-gap 0.69 s (findings §2, Skew). Each switch between the two modules now sends only the three per-module setup lines that `scanMode22Profile` sends for a non-first module, and the context reads leave the bracket.

Each session runs:
1. `init(genericProfile)`, then `scanMode22Profile(session, DRIVE_FAST_PROFILE)`. This is the full setup and the first `2414`/`2885` pair, unchanged.
2. Blocks, numbered `b = 0, 1, …` within the session:
   1. Bare `22 2414`, `22 2885` pairs: `FAST_PAIRS − 1` of them in block 0, which follows step 1's pair, and `FAST_PAIRS` in every later block. The first pair of a later block is the *after* current of the previous group read.
   2. When `b % CONTEXT_EVERY === 0`: `switchTo("CB")`, then `22 2AF5`, `22 2B43`, `22 2AF1`, then `switchTo("17")`.
   3. `22 2414`, the *before* current.
   4. `switchTo("CB")`, then `22 2AE1` … `22 2AE7`, then `switchTo("17")`.

`switchTo(tt)` sends `ATSH DA<tt>F1`, `ATCRA 18DAF1<tt>` and `ATFCSH 18DA<tt>F1`. Each must answer `OK`; otherwise `ElmSessionError("init", …)` is thrown, as `scanMode22Profile`'s `setup` does. Stops are checked after every pair and after step 4, as in T2.12.

Expected on the phone, from the command times in findings §2 (about 0.042 s per AT command, 0.078 s per `22` on `17`, 0.107 s per `22` on `CB`):
- before-gap about 0.21 s;
- after-gap about 0.24 s;
- bracket about 1.1 s, against drive 1's 1.843 s;
- a group set about every 2.7 s.

These are estimates. Stage B measures the real values.

`FAST_PAIRS` stays 10. `CONTEXT_EVERY = 10` blocks (about 30 s) is a policy value: SOC and the raw temperature change slowly during a drive. The first block in every session reads them.

### `7F 22 21`: one re-send (A2)

Drive 1 has 39 `7F 22 21` replies (36 from `CB` as `18DAF1CB 03 7F 22 21`, 3 from module `17` on `2414`/`2885`) and drive 2 has 1 (findings §3, answer 2). On drive 1 these replies dropped 17 group sets. The drive's `CycleSession` re-sends a `22 <DID>` command once, immediately, when its reply contains a frame with `negative = { service: 0x22, code: 0x21 }`. Only the second reply is passed to the builder. A second `7F 22 21` is kept as it is, and the builder drops the set as it does today.

Live capture and replay agree on this:
- `chargeLogFromRecording` already skips an attempt whose next tx in the segment is the same command (T2.4 Decision 18).
- The A2 schedule never sends the same `22` twice in a row except as a re-send.

The re-send is a read through the same guard (hard rule 5). Mode 22 is never answered by a session change. The charge log does not get the re-send: `CycleSession` gains a constructor flag that defaults to off.

**Meaning of code 0x21.** No file in the repo defines it. `docs/ELM327.md` §J1979 conventions says decoders report the code byte only, and T0.5 and T0.7 make "no NRC interpretation" a non-goal. Hard rule 1 therefore blocks writing a meaning until a source is pinned (prerequisite A2-0, open question 1). The re-send itself rests on the recordings: the code appears on 1 to 5 DIDs in a dropped set, and later sets of the same DIDs complete (findings §3). It does not rest on the code's meaning.

### 10-minute option (A2)

`runDriveCapture(deps, minutes)` takes `10 | 20`:
- The cap is `minutes × 60` s.
- The stop reason is `"<minutes> min recorded"`.
- The status line is `Test drive: m:ss of <minutes>:00 recorded; …`.

Developer tools shows two buttons, **Record test drive (10 min)** and **Record test drive (20 min)**, each gated as today. The running card's text names the chosen length. The meta note is `T2.13 test drive (<minutes> min): <note>`. Everything else in `testDrive()` is unchanged.

### Stage B: confirming drive (owner)

One drive on the Equinox with the A2 build, 10 or 20 minutes as the owner chooses. The drive passes when:
1. the redacted recording replays;
2. `## Groups under load` reads `checked` with no flag;
3. the Skew bracket median is below drive 1's 1.843 s.

If item 2 fails (a flag, or `not-checked`) on this healthy car, the rule failed its confirmation. The owner then chooses between:
- shipping the drive check as "not checked" (PLAN T2.13 acceptance);
- a new pre-registered rule in a new task.

Either way, the rule is not edited here.

### Stage C: drive report and codes rescan (revised 2026-10-24)

**Stage B does not change the shape of Stage C.** Everything Stage C builds, the rescan, the comparison, the report type and its text, is independent of whether the rule is confirmed. Only one value depends on Stage B: the rule-status label, a constant in `driveReport.ts` that is `"unconfirmed"` when Stage C merges and becomes `"confirmed"` in a one-line change after Stage B passes. If Stage B fails, the owner chooses (Stage B section); a "withdrawn" state is then a small new task, not built now. Stage C is safe to build on the two committed drives because nothing in it touches the rule or its constants, and the drive button stays in Developer tools (owner only) until T2.18, so no tester sees a group finding before Stage B.

**Rule-status label.** While the status is `"unconfirmed"`, every rendering of the groups part (markdown, result card, saved JSON field `groupRule`) carries the line `Group rule: not yet confirmed on a second drive (project policy, set from one car).` An unflagged result reads `No group stood out in this drive. Provisional until the rule is confirmed.` A flagged result reads `Group <n> (module <m>) sagged more than the others. Provisional until the rule is confirmed; get it inspected.` The text never uses "passed", "validated" or "no red flags" (T2.15 owns those words). The label sits beside the finding, not in a footnote. The saved report stores `groupRule`, so a report made before the flip keeps its label.

1. **Report type.** `DriveReport` is a new type in `packages/obd-battery/src/driveReport.ts`, parsed with zod at its boundary (`parseDriveReport`), version 1. `BatteryDiagnosisReport` and `report.ts` do not change. It holds the garage entry id, catalog id, `scannedAt` (the drive stop time), the drive recording name, `minutes`, `stopReason`, `groupRule`, the `GroupsUnderLoad` result (without `medianGroupMohm` in the rendered text; the field stays in the saved JSON per Design item 6), `codes` (a `CodesComparison` or `{ status: "not-run", reason }`), and `preDrive?: { scannedAt; recording }` pointing at the compared parked report.
2. **Entry point.** `driveReportFromRecording(jsonl, input, preDrive?)` replays the drive recording through `chargeLogFromRecording` and `groupsUnderLoad`, and reads the rescan from the meta `{ event: "codes rescan" }` onward with `codesReportFromRecording`. A recording with no such meta (both committed drives, and any Stage B drive made on the A2 build) gives `codes: { status: "not-run", reason: "no codes rescan in this recording" }`. Same pattern as `batteryDiagnosisFromRecording`, so the replay and the app share one path.
3. **Rescan.** After the drive loop ends, before the link is closed:
   - stop reason is the cap (`"<minutes> min recorded"`) or Disconnect: write meta `{ event: "codes rescan" }`, open a fresh session (`ATZ` first, docs/ELM327.md §Write safety), send `CODES_SCAN_COMMANDS` in the same recording, stopping on `codesScanStop`;
   - car-off or any other stop (including a lost link): no rescan, `codes = not-run, reason "car off"` or the stop reason. Modules do not answer when the car is off (T2.12 §Design, Stop rule);
   - a rescan stopped by `codesScanStop` gives `not-run` with that reason; a partial rescan is compared only for the modules it read, and the text says it stopped early.

   All commands are the existing read-only list; no new command, no write (hard rule 5).
4. **Comparison.** `compareCodes(before, after)` lists, per module (`ecu`), the codes new, gone or unchanged in stored, pending and permanent. A module present in only one report is listed as "not read before" or "not read after", never as all-new or all-gone. No verdict. `before` is the parked report's `codes`.
5. **Pre-drive report.** The app picks the latest saved `BatteryDiagnosisReport` of the same garage entry whose `scannedAt` is earlier than the drive start and on the same device-local calendar day. None gives `preDrive` absent and the text `no pre-drive scan to compare`. The rescan still runs and its codes are listed on their own.
6. **Saving and showing.** `DriveReport` is saved in its own history file (`drive-reports.json`, same atomic-write pattern as the battery reports), so `battery-reports.json` and its parser are untouched. After the drive, the Developer-tools result card shows `renderDriveReport` text. Reopening from the garage is T2.18.

## Risks / open questions

Open questions (ask the owner via the main session):

   - the public AUTOSAR Diagnostic Communication Manager SWS (a named release);
   - `python-udsoncan` at a pinned commit.

   ISO 14229-1 itself is paywalled. Decide which counts as a pinned public source. Also decide whether the re-send ships when none is pinned. My recommendation: yes, because it rests on the recordings, not on the meaning, and the ELM327.md bullet then says "meaning not sourced".

That still meets PLAN's "no flag", but it says nothing about the 10-minute option. Decide whether that is acceptable, or whether Stage B should be a 10-minute drive so that the shorter length is the one confirmed.
4. **10-minute option UI.** Two Developer-tools buttons (proposed), or one button with a length choice?
5. **Drop `2AF5` from every block.** It moves to the every-10th-block context read. T2.12 read it every block to see whether it could replace the seven group reads; that question is now moot under ADR-023. Confirm.
6. **Stage C shape.** Answered: separate drive report (Decisions). Pre-drive scan: same garage entry, same device-local day (proposed; the owner did not object).
7. **Stage C (2026-10-24 revision).** Four product points with recommended defaults, all safe to build on (each is one constant or one string): (a) the label text above; (b) same-day pre-drive match, versus a 24 h window; (c) rescan also on Disconnect; (d) one owner drive on the Stage C build serving as both Stage B and H-C, versus two drives. Defaults: as written, same-day, yes, yes.

Risks:
- **Drive 1 might be `not-checked`**, with too few loaded steady sets. Then no L can be set: A1 stops and escalates, and the check ships as "not checked" (PLAN acceptance). The A2 schedule improves the steady fraction only for future drives.
- **Temperature differences between modules.** A cooler module sags more and could be flagged. There is no °C until T2.17, which is one reason for ADR-023's "get it inspected" wording.
- **L comes from one healthy car.** Its sensitivity is shown only on injected faults (ADR-023, Limits). The G2/G3 artifacts are synthetic and stay in a separate table.
- **The shorter switch** depends on `ATCP`/`ATFCSD`/`ATFCSM` persisting across the switch. The fallback is in §Sources, and Stage B shows it on the car.
- **The re-send may cluster.** Drive 1's codes clustered early in the drive (findings §3). If a burst repeats, the re-sends lengthen a bracket, and `BRACKET_MAX_S` or `STEADY_A` drop that set rather than mis-measure it.
